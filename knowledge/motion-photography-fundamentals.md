Panning follows a moving subject with the camera during the exposure. The goal
is often to keep an important part of the subject relatively sharp while the
background becomes streaked or blurred, communicating speed. Success depends
on both shutter speed and smooth movement with the subject.[^nikon-panning][^canon-panning]

## Salman's experience and intended result

Salman wants to create a beautiful panning photograph of a moving motorcycle
or car. He wants the rider or driver to appear relatively frozen and sharp
while the background looks fast and moving. Movement in the wheels or tires
can also help communicate speed.[^salman-motion-interview-2026-09-18]

His physical method is to rotate his body together with the camera while
following the subject. He tries to keep the rider in roughly the same position
in the frame as the background moves.[^salman-motion-interview-2026-09-18]

Salman takes many photographs while rotating with the moving subject, then
selects the frame that came out best. He stands in one safe position, observes
whether the subject is traveling from left to right or right to left, and
rotates his body and camera in that direction.[^salman-motion-interview-2026-09-18]

In his experience, some phones may offer dedicated panning or motion modes
under different names. With a dedicated camera, he adjusts for the available
light by considering shutter speed, ISO and aperture together.
[^salman-motion-interview-2026-09-18]

These paragraphs record Salman's reported experience only. The procedure below
adds expert-supported techniques; it does not claim that Salman has already
used every one of them.

## Expert-supported panning procedure

### Observe and prepare

Watch the subject's path and direction before taking the photograph. Choose a
safe fixed position from which the movement can be followed smoothly. A
predictable path also makes it easier to decide where focus should be acquired
and where the strongest part of the picture may happen.
[^salman-motion-interview-2026-09-18][^nikon-panning]

### Choose how motion should look

Shutter speed is the main creative control for motion rendering. A shorter
exposure holds more subject detail; a longer exposure can create more
background and wheel blur but also makes keeping the subject sharp more
difficult. There is no single universal shutter speed because subject speed,
direction, distance, lens choice, available light and the intended amount of
blur all matter.[^nikon-panning][^canon-panning]

Where controls are available, aperture and ISO balance the exposure around the
chosen shutter speed. They retain their usual trade-offs in depth of field and
image noise; see [the Exposure fundamentals guide](exposure-fundamentals.md).
The device may make some or all of these choices automatically.

### Choose a focus approach

For a subject moving unpredictably or changing distance, continuous or
tracking autofocus can keep adjusting focus while the photographer follows it,
where the device offers that feature.[^nikon-panning][^sony-af-modes]

For a predictable path, an expert-supported alternative is to pre-focus on a
zone the subject will cross and make the photograph as it reaches that zone.
This reduces the need for the focus system to follow the subject throughout
the pass.[^nikon-panning]

### Follow smoothly through the frame

Begin moving with the subject before releasing the shutter. Rotate smoothly
and try to keep the chosen part of the subject in a consistent position in the
frame. Release the shutter without stopping the movement, then continue the
pan afterward. This follow-through avoids turning the shutter press into an
abrupt stop.[^nikon-panning][^canon-panning]

### Make several attempts and inspect them

Panning has a low keeper rate even for experienced photographers. Make several
frames when the situation allows, then compare which parts are sharp, how the
background flows and whether the amount of wheel or body blur supports the
intended sense of speed. Practice with repeatable movement and experiment with
different shutter speeds rather than expecting one setting to work for every
subject.[^nikon-panning][^canon-panning]

## A simple mental model

- **Match movement:** rotate with the subject so its position changes less in
  the frame.
- **Let the background move:** the exposure records the background sweeping
  across the frame.
- **Protect one anchor:** keep an important part, such as the rider or driver,
  as the visual point of sharpness.
- **Expect variation:** subject speed, distance, light and panning smoothness
  change the result from one pass to the next.

Phones and dedicated cameras expose different controls and may use different
names for panning modes, continuous focus or tracking. This guide describes
the photographic method, not a model-specific menu path.
